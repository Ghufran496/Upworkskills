# Pricing strategy

Goal: a number that wins the job AND respects the freelancer's floor rate (from `assets/profile.md`). Never race to the bottom.

## Read the budget signal
- **Fixed-price post with a budget** → anchor near it, but justify value. If the budget is too low for the scope, say so and propose a smaller phase 1.
- **Hourly post with a range** → quote at or slightly above the freelancer's normal rate if the job matches their strength; the top of a client's range often signals they want quality.
- **No budget listed** → give a range or a "starting at" with what changes it. Never leave it open-ended.

## Framing that wins
- Price the outcome, not the hours: *"$1,200 to get checkout live and converting"* beats *"$40/hr for ~30 hrs"*.
- Offer a small, cheap **Phase 1** to lower risk: *"$300 to audit + fix the top 3 issues this week; we scope the rest after you see results."* This is the single highest-converting move for cautious clients.
- Anchor with one number, then a reason. Don't stack discounts.
- If undercutting to land a first review in a niche, say it's an intro rate so you can raise later.

## Estimating scope (phased)
1. Break the job into 2–4 phases/deliverables.
2. Attach hours or a flat price to each based on the freelancer's real speed.
3. Add a buffer (15–25%) for revisions/unknowns.
4. Present as a small table or 2–3 lines, not a spreadsheet.

## How to state the price in the proposal
- One line, confident, in the Close. *"Fixed $900, delivered in 5 days, one revision round included."*
- Include what's in scope so it can't be nibbled: deliverables + revision rounds + timeline.
- If unsure of scope, quote Phase 1 firmly and say the rest is scoped after.

## Floors (never break)
- Never go below the minimum rate in `assets/profile.md`.
- Never quote a fixed price on an undefined scope — quote a paid discovery/audit first.
