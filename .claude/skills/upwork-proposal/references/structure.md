# Proposal structure — Hook / Proof / Close

Every proposal has three parts. Nothing else is required. Keep the whole thing **80–150 words** unless the job is enterprise/complex.

## 1. HOOK (1–2 sentences)
Open with something specific from THIS job post — the client's actual problem, a detail only someone who read it would mention, or a sharp observation about their goal. Never open with "Hi, I'm a [role] with X years of experience."

Good hook patterns:
- Name the real problem behind the task: *"Your checkout drop-off isn't a design problem — it's the 3-step form. That's fixable this week."*
- React to a specific detail: *"You mentioned the old dev disappeared mid-project. First thing I'd do is audit what's actually shippable so you're not paying twice."*
- Ask one sharp question that proves expertise: *"Quick one — is the Stripe integration already live, or are we building it? That changes the whole timeline."*

## 2. PROOF (1–3 sentences)
ONE concrete, relevant past outcome. Numbers > adjectives. Pull from `assets/profile.md`. Tie it to their problem.
- *"I rebuilt a checkout like this for a Shopify store last month — drop-off went from 68% to 41% in two weeks."*
- If no perfect match, use the closest real one and connect the dots. Never invent.

## 3. CLOSE (1–2 sentences)
A specific, low-friction next step. Make saying yes easy.
- *"Want me to send a 3-step plan and a fixed quote today? Reply and I'll have it to you in an hour."*
- *"I can start Monday. Happy to do a quick 15-min call first if useful."*
- Avoid "Looking forward to hearing from you" — it's filler and signals a mass-bid.

## Screening questions
If the post has them, answer separately, concretely, one line each. These are often the real filter — a great proposal with skipped questions loses.

## Hidden instructions
Some clients bury a rule ("begin your proposal with the word 'banana'"). If present, obey it in the very first line. It's a filter to kill copy-paste bidders.

## Length by job type
- Small/quick gig: 60–100 words.
- Standard project: 100–150 words.
- Enterprise/complex: up to 250, but structured with a short "How I'd approach it" list.

## What kills a proposal (never do)
- Generic opener about yourself.
- Wall of text / your life story.
- Listing every skill you have instead of the ones this job needs.
- Vague claims with no number or example.
- No clear next step.
