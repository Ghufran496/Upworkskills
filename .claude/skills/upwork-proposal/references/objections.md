# Objection handling & follow-ups

Reply fast, stay confident, remove the risk. Never get defensive or discount reflexively.

## Common objections → responses

**"Your price is too high."**
Don't just drop the price. Re-anchor on outcome or offer a smaller phase.
- *"Totally fair. The full build is $X because it includes [scope]. If budget's tight, I can do a $Y phase 1 that gets [core result] live first — you decide on the rest after you see it work."*

**"Can you do it cheaper / for $Z?"**
- If above floor: trade scope for price, don't just cut. *"I can hit $Z if we drop [feature] for now and add it in a phase 2."*
- If below floor: hold. *"I can't go that low and still do it right, but here's the most I can get you for that budget: [reduced scope]."*

**"Do you have experience with X specifically?"**
- If yes: one concrete example. If adjacent: bridge honestly. *"Not X exactly, but I've done Y which is the same underlying problem — here's how it maps."* Never claim experience you don't have.

**"Why should I pick you over cheaper bidders?"**
- *"Cheaper usually means you pay twice. I'll get it right the first time and I'm here after launch. Happy to prove it with a small paid first step so it's low-risk for you."*

**"Can we hop on a call first?"**
- Always yes, fast. *"Sure — I'm free [times]. Send a slot or I'll propose one."* Confirm the time zone.

**"I need it faster."**
- Be honest about what's possible. Offer a rushed phase 1 or a clear timeline. Never promise what you can't ship.

## Follow-ups (no response)
- Wait ~2–3 days. Keep it short, add value, no guilt-trip.
- *"Hi [name] — still happy to help with [project]. One idea since I last wrote: [specific tip]. Want me to send a quick plan?"*
- Max 2 follow-ups, then let it go.

## Tone rules
- Never apologize for your rate.
- Never send a wall of text in a reply — 2–4 sentences.
- Always end with a specific next step or question.
- No banned words (see `voice.md`).
